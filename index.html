RESEARCH NOTES

Data & methodology

Martian Map combines mission context with a transparent, interactive planning prototype. Data is labelled by origin: NASA DATA is real NASA imagery/elevation; ESTIMATED is a heuristic demonstration score. Risk scores are not operational safety assessments.

How each source informs the exploration decision
SOURCETYPEROLE IN DECISION
NASA Mars Trek · MGS MOLA colorized relief (WMTS tiles)NASA IMAGERYShows real global topography so users see where a site sits relative to lowlands, highlands and canyons.
NASA Mars Trek · Viking MDIM 2.1 color mosaicNASA IMAGERYGives surface appearance (albedo, craters, dust) for visual context.
MOLA DEM elevation and slope: raster sampling by the local backend, or a live image-service queryNASA-DERIVEDMeasured height retrieved from the DEM; very high or deep locations are a first flag for entry, descent and traverse difficulty. If the query fails, an approximate reference is shown and labelled as such.
Slope / rock / thermal scores and route risk indexESTIMATED ILLUSTRATIVE zonesHeuristic 0–100 demo values that let users compare sites; they should be replaced by real hazard products.
  1. Inspect the NASA basemap and elevation of each candidate region.
  2. Compare the estimated slope, rock and thermal scores alongside each site's overall terrain-risk score. These are preset demonstration values; they are not computed from the NASA data.
  3. Generate a traverse: distance is great-circle; route risk averages the two sites' scores, adjusted by the chosen avoidance options. Exact formula: route risk = mean of the two endpoint risk scores − 8 (if "Avoid steep terrain") − 5 (if "Minimize rocky zones") + 0.08 × latitude difference in degrees, rounded and limited to 8–99.
  4. Treat the output as a shortlist to validate with high-resolution data (e.g. HiRISE, CTX) before any real decision.

Backend: elevation, DEM slope and terrain routing

Elevation. A small Python (Flask) service opens the USGS "Mars MGS MOLA DEM 463m" GeoTIFF and returns the height of the pixel covering a site's latitude/longitude (pixel column = ⌊(lon + 180) × 128⌋, row = ⌊(90 − lat) × 128⌋). Slope. Computed from the 3×3 neighbouring native cells with Horn's method, using the true cell size in metres at that latitude. This is NASA-derived and separate from the estimated "Slope exposure" score, which is unchanged. Route. A least-cost (A*) search over a block-averaged copy of the DEM, then smoothed into straight legs. A step costs its length × (1 + (slope along the step ÷ 3°)² + ½ (cell slope ÷ 3°)²) with "Avoid steep terrain" on (8° reference when off); steps steeper than 20° (30° when off) or into no-data cells are not allowed. The direct great-circle line is scored with the same cost model and the cheaper one is returned. Limits. Slopes are grid-scale averages over several kilometres, so local slopes, rocks, sand and thermal conditions are not captured; "Minimize rocky zones" affects only the estimated risk index, not the path. Without the backend the app shows the original straight-line draft.

How coordinates are matched to elevation

Each site keeps its fixed latitude/longitude (decimal degrees, east-positive longitude). The app sends that point to the DEM image service's identify operation, which returns the value of the single DEM pixel (about 463 m across) covering the point; nothing is averaged or interpolated. Services differ in longitude convention, so the app tries −180…180 first, then 0…360. Returned values outside −9,000…+22,000 m are rejected as invalid. Because the result is one cell, it can differ from published landing-site elevations and does not describe the local slope or relief.

How NASA data influences exploration decisions

Imagery gives visual context for where a candidate sits (basins, highlands, canyons). Elevation is shown beside the estimated risk so a person can spot extreme depth or height (for example Olympus Mons) and ask for closer study. NASA data is not an input to the risk formulas: terrain-risk, slope, rock and thermal scores are preset demonstration values, and route risk uses only the formula above. The output is a shortlist to validate with high-resolution products (HiRISE, CTX), not an operational decision.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

About Martian Map

Martian Map is an interactive concept for exploring candidate regions on Mars and thinking through the factors that influence a surface traverse.

What you can do

  • Inspect five notable Mars regions and their mission context.
  • Switch between NASA Mars Trek basemaps (MOLA elevation, Viking color) and toggle illustrative slope, rock and thermal overlays.
  • Generate a draft route and compare a simple risk index.
  • Export a mission-plan JSON file for further analysis.

Important: the basemap tiles and site elevations come from NASA datasets (Mars Trek / MGS MOLA). Hazard overlays, risk scores and routes are clearly labelled illustrative estimates. This prototype does not use live telemetry or replace validated elevation, imagery, radiation or weather products.
